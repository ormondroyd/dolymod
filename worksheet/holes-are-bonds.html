KS3 chemistry. Use the free online model kit at molecules.fyi (no login needed). Every atom is a ball with holes, and each hole is one bond. A molecule is finished when every hole is filled.

How to use the kit
  • Pick an atom from the box. Click the empty table to put it down, or click an atom to plug it in.
  • Fill with H puts a hydrogen in every empty hole. Undo takes it back. Clear empties the table.
  • Link: drag from one atom to another to join them. Do it again to make a double bond.
  • The panel under the model shows the formula and the name of what you made.

Answers for teachers

1. Hydrogen 1 hole. Oxygen 2 holes: water, H2O. Nitrogen 3 holes: ammonia, NH3. Carbon 4 holes: methane, CH4. Oxygen has only two holes, so it can make only two bonds.

2. 1: methane, CH4. 2: ethane, C2H6. 3: propane, C3H8. 4: butane, C4H10. 5: pentane, C5H12. A chain of n carbons has 2n + 2 hydrogens. 10 carbons: C10H22 (decane).

3. Butane and isobutane (its IUPAC name, 2-methylpropane, shows under it). The atoms are joined in a different order, so they are different substances: butane boils at about −1 °C and isobutane at about −12 °C.

4. Ethylene, C2H4 (the kit shows its IUPAC name, ethene, under it). The single bond in ethane twists. The double bond won't, and the kit says “Double and triple bonds can't twist”.

Challenge. Two links each: two double bonds, O=C=O.
